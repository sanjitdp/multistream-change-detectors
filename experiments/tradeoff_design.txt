PRE-SPECIFIED CROSS-STREAM ERROR / IDENTIFICATION COMPARISON
Recorded before outcomes were simulated, 2026-10-06.

Purpose: compare methods that all report a subset of the same streams.
The main figure contrasts global-null error with identification of genuine
changes. No global-alarm-only procedure is treated as an identification rule.

DGP: K=100 independent Gaussian streams with unit variance. H=400, change
at observation101, known positive mean shift mu=.5. Configurations m=0,10,50
changed streams; m=0 is global null, others are partial nulls. 4000 independent
replications per configuration. Seeds 61006200+m. Same observations supplied
to all methods within each replication; different seeds across configurations.
Use exact known-model likelihood factors exp(mu X-mu^2/2) and SR recursion.
No empirical threshold tuning or selecting cases after viewing outcomes.

q=.05. Main figure includes these three methods:
1. e-d-BH (ours), alpha=q/H, rank-r threshold K H/(q r).
   EOP implies FDR<=q at EVERY reporting time tau<=H.
2. Parallel SR, threshold K H/q for each stream, i.e. e-d-Bonferroni.
   EOP implies PFER<=q (and FWER<=q) at EVERY tau<=H.
3. Per-stream SR, threshold H/q for each stream.
   For each stream separately, probability of a false alarm byH<=q;
   this does not impose a q budget on errors across the whole family.
The methods have a common numerical error budget, but different error
criteria. The comparison must say this explicitly, not call them matched
FDR, matched FWER or matched achieved ARL procedures.

An additional fixed-time benchmark is reported in the complete tables:
4. SR/t e-BH (SHEREF), fixed-time levelq, rank-r threshold Kt/(q r).
   Guarantees fixed-time FDR<=q, not arbitrary-stopping-time FDR<=q.
All four methods and all configurations remain in machine-readable results.
SHEREF is omitted from the main figure to keep its distinct stopping-time
target clear; its outcomes are retained in a separate supplemental figure.

Practical reporting rules (no use of true changed-stream identities):
- first time>=1 stream is currently declared, capped atH;
- first time>=10 streams are currently declared, capped atH;
- deterministicH.
At each report: FDR=mean FDP, FWER=probability any false declaration,
PFER=mean false count, reported-set size, TDR=proportion of already changed
streams declared (zero beforenu), stopping time, and raw true count.
Under global null FDR at the first-report rule is exactly probability of
any declaration byH. Detector decisions are current sets, not permanent
unions; capped reporting uses the CURRENT set atH.

Identification performance: current TDR at each time, plus first post-change
time when>=80% and100% of the changed streams are simultaneously declared.
These are oracle diagnostics for an ongoing monitoring rule, not practical
stopping rules and not conditional on no earlier false alarm. Nondetections
are assigned timeH+1; report restricted mean delay AND nondetection fraction.

Main figure, fixed in advance: panel(a) global-null probability of any
declaration byH with q=.05 reference, panel(b) dense m=50 current true-discovery
fraction versus post-change observation for the three main methods. The
identification panel shows first180 post-change observations for readability;
the entire300-observation trajectory is saved. A companion figure shows both
m=10 andm=50 trajectories and allfour methods; all reporting-rule results are
saved irrespective of which method performs best.

Uncertainty: independent replications are sampling units, including all
streams inside each replicate. Plot +/-1.96 Monte Carlo SE (clipped to[0,1]
for probabilities). These are simulation errors, not inferential intervals
for unknown model parameters or simultaneous confidence bands. Retain paired
replicate-level results and exact seeds for reanalysis.

Interpretation to assess rather than assume: BH may identify many changes
sooner than multiplicity-adjusted parallel charts; per-stream thresholds
may be faster but incur substantial family-level false-alarm probability.
SHEREF's target must be distinguished at fixed versus adaptive reports.
These finite simulations do not estimate the EOP supremum or establish
universal delay superiority. No outcome-dependent redesign is authorized.
